1. The scholar's journey, step by step

Every applicant moves through the same eight stops on the way to becoming an enrolled scholar. A person can drop out (be rejected) at any stop along the way — that's normal and expected, not a system error.

📝
Applicant

1. Apply

A candidate fills in the public application form and submits it for the programme they're interested in (POSTDOC or D.Litt).

What happens next: an "Application Received" confirmation email goes out automatically. Status in the system: submitted

🧪
Coordinator

2. Test

The coordinator reviews the application and assigns an entrance test. The applicant gets a login link and a temporary username/password by email, valid for 5 days from when they first click it, then logs in and sits the test.

What happens next: an "Entrance Test Credentials" email goes out, and a reminder email can be sent if the applicant hasn't logged in yet. Statuses in the system: shortlisted_test (invited) → test_pending (credentials sent, awaiting the sitting) → test_completed (result recorded)

✅
Coordinator

3. Shortlist

The coordinator reviews the test score and moves the strongest candidates onto the Final Shortlist for the batch.

What happens next: a "Qualified for Interview Stage" email goes out automatically, stating the registration fee (USD 200 / INR 19,000) with bank details, and that a Personal Interview will be scheduled separately. Status in the system: shortlisted

💳
Applicant

4. Registration fee

The shortlisted candidate pays the registration fee (USD 200 / INR 19,000) by bank transfer to confirm their seat.

This is a waiting period on top of the shortlisted status, not a separate status of its own.

💰
Coordinator

5. Payment confirmed

The coordinator confirms the fee has cleared and marks the applicant as paid.

Status in the system: payment_received

🗣️
Coordinator

6. Personal interview

The coordinator schedules and holds the Personal Interview mentioned in the shortlist email, then decides whether to confirm the candidate.

This happens outside the applicant's payment_received status — there's no separate "interviewed" status, it's a manual step the coordinator completes before converting the applicant.

🎓
Coordinator

7. Enrollment

Once the interview confirms the candidate, the coordinator clicks "Convert to Student," which creates the scholar's account, enrolls them into the specific batch (e.g. "Batch Dec 2025"), and — by default — emails them fresh Scholar Portal login credentials.

Status in the system: enrolled

📚
Scholar

8. Ongoing work begins

The scholar now submits Assignments and twice-yearly Progress Reports for the rest of the programme. See the next section for how that works.

⚠️

Rejection can happen at any stop. A candidate who doesn't clear the test, isn't shortlisted, doesn't pay the fee, or doesn't pass interview is marked rejected rather than being deleted — their record is kept, just closed out.

🛤️

Not every batch takes this full path. Batch Dec 2025 was onboarded directly rather than run through the entrance-test funnel — all 23 of its scholars were enrolled without stopping at the intermediate statuses above. The full Apply → Test → Interview funnel is what's currently playing out for Batch June 2026.

2. What happens after enrollment

Once enrolled, a scholar's obligations fall into two different kinds of submission that work quite differently from each other — this trips people up, so it's worth spelling out.

📄 Assignments 📈 Progress reports 🎯 Targets
What it is Coursework tasks issued during a semester A twice-yearly (6-monthly) review of the scholar's research work Specific milestones the scholar is tracked against
Sign-off needed? No — submitting it is the final step Yes — goes through a review chain before it's accepted Yes — one approver signs it off
What gets uploaded Any number of files, mixed formats Exactly two files: a PDF write-up and a PPT presentation One or more supporting files
How often Several per semester Once per semester (every 6 months) Several per semester
💡

In plain terms: an Assignment is "hand it in and you're done." A Progress Report is "hand it in, then a reviewer reads it and either approves it or sends it back with feedback" — much closer to a thesis check-in than a homework drop-box.

4. Known gaps and what they mean

Two things worth flagging honestly rather than papering over.

  • Document-upload tracking doesn't exist yet. There's no field anywhere in the applicant, scholar, or research-profile data that counts "documents uploaded during application." If a coordinator asks "how many applicants have uploaded their ID proof / marksheets," that number can't currently be pulled from the system — it isn't that the data was hidden or missed, the field simply hasn't been built.
  • The Batch June 2026 enrollment count reads as 1, not 0, in raw exports. That single enrolled record was later withdrawn, so anyone skimming a raw table (rather than this snapshot) could mistakenly think the batch has already started. It hasn't.
